## Overview

The Yahwist Liberation Hermeneutic is a polyphonic interpretive methodology that reads texts as sites of **competing systems** shaped by distinct social, political, and ideological conditions.

It was developed initially as a framework for biblical analysis — specifically for reading Hebrew scripture after stripping away the Greek epistemological overlays imposed through centuries of Western theological translation. But its analytical operations are not dependent on religious subject matter.

**YLH reads any text by consequence, not intent.**

Meaning is assessed not by what a text claims to value, but by what it structurally produces: how it shapes moral imagination, organizes social relations, and distributes or concentrates power.

---

## Core Principle: Consequence Over Coherence

Most interpretive frameworks ask: *Is this text internally consistent? Does it cohere with an established standard?*

YLH asks: *What does this text produce when followed? Who benefits from the outcomes it generates? What must be true for its proposals to work — and who bears the cost when they don't?*

This is not cynicism about authorial intent. Authors can be sincere and their frameworks can still encode domination. Sincerity is not a structural category. Consequence is.

## The Five Analytical Operations

### Operation 1: Consequence Mapping

**Question:** What does this text actually produce?

Trace each major proposal or claim to its downstream outcome — not the stated goal, but the structural result given the mechanisms proposed. A wealth fund with no mandatory contribution architecture produces voluntary philanthropy, not universal dividend, regardless of what it is called.

Useful diagnostic questions:
- Who holds decision-making authority over this mechanism?
- What happens when the mechanism fails or is resisted?
- Who bears the cost of failure?
- Does the outcome match the stated goal — or does the language of the goal obscure what the mechanism actually does?

---

### Operation 2: Algorithm Identification

**Question:** What is the underlying logic that drives these outcomes?

Every text encodes a decision logic — a set of rules that, when followed, produce the outcomes the text generates. Identifying the algorithm makes the logic explicit and testable.

Algorithm identification asks:
- What is the chain of reasoning from premise to conclusion?
- What rules must be followed to arrive at the proposed outcomes?
- Does the stated algorithm match the operative algorithm — or does the text say one thing while its logic does another?
- What other outputs would this algorithm produce if applied consistently?

In YLH's original biblical context, this operation identifies whether a textual tradition encodes liberation (resistance to domination, affirmation of human dignity) or stabilization (hierarchy maintenance, ritualized control, imperial order). In secular policy contexts, the same operation identifies whether a document's logic produces distribution or concentration.

---

### Operation 3: Substrate Excavation

**Question:** What foundational assumptions does this text cannot question without collapsing?

Every framework rests on commitments it treats as settled — not argued for, but assumed. These are the load-bearing walls. Substrate excavation makes them visible.

Diagnostic moves:
- What does the text assert as obvious or beyond debate?
- What positions are foreclosed before the argument begins?
- What questions cannot be asked within this framework?
- If the substrate assumption is removed, does the policy architecture still stand?

Substrate is not the same as bias. Bias implies error. Substrate is structural — it is the ground the argument stands on. Recognizing substrate is not a refutation; it is a map of what the framework can and cannot do.

---

### Operation 4: Competing Cosmologies Detection

**Question:** Whose worldview is centered — and whose is rendered invisible or impossible?

Every text encodes a cosmology: a set of assumptions about what is real, what is valuable, what counts as a legitimate claim, and who counts as a legitimate actor. Competing cosmologies detection identifies the dominant cosmology and surfaces the worldviews it suppresses, silences, or renders unthinkable.

This operation is particularly important for texts that claim universality. "People first" and "benefits everyone" are cosmological claims. They describe a world in which "people" and "everyone" are already defined — and that definition always excludes someone.

Key questions:
- Who is assumed to be the primary actor in this text?
- Whose experience is treated as the default?
- What communities, perspectives, or value systems are absent — not argued against, but simply not present?
- What would this text look like if written from the excluded perspective?

---

### Operation 5: Counter-Algorithm Specification

**Question:** What would a liberation-aligned alternative require structurally?

This operation is not idealism. It is specification. If the document's algorithm produces concentration, what algorithm produces distribution? If the document's mechanisms are voluntary, what mechanisms are mandatory? If the document forecloses certain questions, what framework opens them?

Counter-algorithm specification is the constructive output of the analysis. It does not demand perfection — it identifies the minimum structural requirements for the stated goals to be achievable by the proposed means.

## The Multi-Axis Scoring Rubric

YLH analyses score texts on five axes, each rated 1–5:

| Axis | What It Measures | 1 (Low) | 5 (High) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Alignment** | Do mechanisms match stated goals? | Deep contradiction | Full alignment |
| **Liberation** | Does the text move toward or away from liberation outcomes? | Actively harmful | Structurally liberating |
| **Cosmological Load** | How heavy is the ideological freight? | Transparent assumptions | Dense, hidden ideology |
| **Substrate** | What system does the text assume? | Capitalist/imperial | Pluralist/relational |
| **Algorithmic Function** | What does the operative logic produce? | Distortion | Liberation |

**Algorithmic Function Categories:**
- **Distortion** — Uses liberation language while encoding domination mechanisms
- **Depth Perception** — Partially liberating; contains genuine and harmful elements in tension
- **Illusion** — Presents domination as natural, inevitable, or divinely ordained

## On Theological and Secular Translation

YLH was developed through biblical analysis. Its vocabulary — substrate, cosmology, algorithm, Yahwist tradition, counter-algorithm — comes from that context.

But the operations are not dependent on that vocabulary.

"Substrate excavation" in theological language is "hidden assumptions audit" in policy language. "Competing cosmologies detection" is "stakeholder exclusion analysis." "Counter-algorithm specification" is "alternative mechanism design."

The translation is not a simplification. It is a demonstration that the methodology is portable — that the same analytical moves work across domains because they are tracking the same underlying phenomena: how texts organize power, whose interests they serve, and what they structurally cannot do.

This repository publishes both versions of every analysis to make that translation visible. Coalition work across communities requires shared analytical capacity. Shared analytical capacity requires that the tools be accessible in multiple registers.

## Intellectual Lineage

YLH draws on and is in conversation with:

- **Source Criticism** — Documentary Hypothesis (Wellhausen, Cross, Friedman); Yahwist, Elohist, Deuteronomist, and Priestly traditions as ideologically distinct
- **Liberation Theology** — King, Cone, Thurman, Williams, Schüssler Fiorenza; consequence as the measure of theological truth
- **Decolonial Theory** — Quijano, Mignolo, Jennings; coloniality of knowledge as governance architecture
- **Hebrew Linguistics** — *naphesh*, *tselem*, *mishpat*, *shalom* as relational categories distinct from their Greek translations
- **Pragmatism** — Dewey; meaning determined by consequences, not abstract coherence
- **Walter Benjamin** — History read from the perspective of the defeated, not the victors
